# Content-Rights Notice and Takedown Process

[简体中文](NOTICE_AND_TAKEDOWN.md) | **English**

This process covers concerns about copyright, licensing, likeness, privacy, or the scope of
consent. It is not legal advice and does not mean the project respects rights only after a
notice. Notice and takedown cannot replace prior authorization.

## Reporting a concern

Use the repository's **Rights or privacy concern / 内容权利问题** issue form and provide:

- the repository path, commit, release, or exact URL at issue;
- your relationship to the relevant rights;
- a concise description of the right or authorization scope;
- the action requested; and
- confirmation that the report is accurate and submitted in good faith.

Do not post identity documents, signed agreements, non-public contact details, source photos,
or other sensitive material in a public issue. If private evidence is necessary, first send a
minimal summary through a private contact method listed on the repository owner's GitHub
profile. Formal copyright notices about GitHub-hosted content may also use GitHub's current
content-removal channel.

## Maintainer response

1. Record the receipt date, reported location, and current file or commit hash.
2. Quarantine, hide, or pause distribution when the concern is credible and needs review.
3. Check provenance, license version, permission evidence, subject authorization, and actual
   use.
4. Remove, replace, restrict, correct required attribution, or restore the item according to
   the evidence.
5. Check copies from the same source, release artifacts, and derived manifests so the response
   is not limited to one visible link.
6. Keep a non-sensitive disposition record and respond to the reporter where appropriate.

The project aims to acknowledge a notice within seven days, although fact-finding, identity
checks, and platform processes may take longer. Temporary quarantine is not an admission of
infringement; restoration still requires verifiable authorization. Disputed material cannot
count toward G3, enter a distribution artifact, or support product claims.

If content reached Git history or a release, deleting the working-tree file may be insufficient.
Maintainers will assess release withdrawal, artifact deletion, or the hosting platform's
history-removal process. Any destructive history rewrite requires a defined scope and an audit
record first.
